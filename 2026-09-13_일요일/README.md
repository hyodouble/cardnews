# Why Korea Needs a Law to Send Children Home at Night

- 발행 예정: 2026-09-13 (일요일)

## 캡션 — 영어판 (그대로 복사)

```
At ten at night in a Korean city, a side street empties out of children. They are not coming from school. They are coming from hagwon — private academies that teach the school subjects over again, after school and at the weekend.

Three in four school children take some form of private tutoring. Last year the country spent ₩27.5tn, about $19bn, on private tutoring: ₩458,000 a month averaged across every child, and ₩793,000 for a high schooler who actually goes.

Ten o'clock is not a habit, it is the law. Since 2008, local ordinances have capped how late an academy may teach — 10pm in Seoul, Gyeonggi, Daegu and Gwangju, 11pm in Busan and Incheon, midnight in Daejeon and Ulsan — and education offices run night patrols before exams to enforce it.

The cap has a door in it. A study cafe is not an academy, so at 10:01pm the children move next door and keep going.

Last year the total fell for the first time in five years. It was reported as a warning, not a win.
```

## 확인이 필요한 사실

1. 'three in four' (1장·캡션): 2025년 초중고 사교육비조사 기준 사교육 참여율 75.7%(전년 대비 4.3%p 감소), 2026년 3월 발표분. 이 수치는 학원만이 아니라 개인과외·학습지를 포함한 사교육 전체 참여율이라, 카드는 '학원 하나 이상'이 아니라 'some form of private tutoring'으로 썼다. 학원 단독 비율로 바꿔 쓰면 틀린 문장이 된다.
2. '₩27.5tn / about $19bn' (5장·캡션): 2025년 사교육비 총액 27조 5천억 원, 전년 대비 1조 7천억 원 감소. 달러 환산은 1,400원/달러 기준 근사치라 'roughly'로 썼다.
3. '₩458,000 / ₩793,000' (5장): 전체학생 1인당 월평균 45만 8천 원(-3.5%), 참여 고등학생 79만 3천 원(+2.6%). 두 숫자의 모집단이 다르므로 카드에서 'across all children'과 'who actually attends'로 구분해 썼다. 섞으면 틀린 문장이 된다.
4. 'Sunday is a working day' (3장): 주말 운영은 일반적이지만 전국 공통 규칙이 아니라 학원별·지역별로 다르다. 'many'로 완화해 썼고 숫자는 붙이지 않았다.
5. 'shuttle buses' (4장): 학원 셔틀 운행은 사실이나 '열 시에 줄지어 선다'는 장면 묘사는 통계가 아니라 관찰이다. 수치 주장으로 읽히지 않게 문장에 숫자를 넣지 않았다(‘약 15분’은 장면 설명).
6. 'since 2008 / city-by-city caps' (6장·캡션): 2008년 서울시교육청이 처음 도입해 시도 조례로 확산. 서울·경기·대구·광주 22시, 부산·인천 23시, 대전·울산 24시. 인천·세종은 초·중·고 학년별로 시간이 갈리는 등 예외가 있어 카드는 대표 도시만 이름을 댔다.
7. 'night patrols before exams' (6장): 중간고사 등 시험 기간을 앞두고 심야교습 집중 점검이 실시된다는 보도 기준. 상시 단속이 아니라 기간 단속이므로 'before exams'를 반드시 붙여야 한다.
8. 'study cafe loophole' (7장): 스터디카페는 학원법상 교습시간 제한 대상이 아니어서 심야까지 운영된다. '10:01pm에 옮겨 간다'는 수사이고, 실제로 규제 공백을 파고든 변칙 사교육이라는 지적이 보도돼 있다.
9. 'Seoul weighing midnight for high schoolers' (8장·첫 댓글): 서울 고등학생 교습시간을 24시까지 연장하는 조례안을 둘러싼 찬반 논의 기준. 통과 여부·시행 시점은 카드에 쓰지 않았다.
10. 'fell for the first time in five years / dropped four points' (9장): 총액 감소와 참여율 4.3%p 감소는 같은 조사 결과다. 다만 참여학생 1인당 금액은 오히려 올라(60만 4천 원, +2%) 양극화로 읽혔다는 해석까지가 카드의 마지막 문장이다.

## 슬라이드 문구

**01. Korea's second school day **ends at ten at night.****
  EN  SUNDAY NIGHT. TEN O'CLOCK. A SIDE STREET FILLS WITH CHILDREN.
  EN  Not school. Hagwon — private academies. Three in four school children take some form of private tutoring.

**02. It has a name: **hagwon****
  EN  A paid academy that teaches the school subjects again, after school and at the weekend. It is not remedial and it is not a club. For most families it is simply the second half of the day.

**03. Sunday is **a working day for it****
  EN  Many academies open at the weekend, because that is when a child has a clear block of hours. In an exam month a Sunday can run longer than a Monday.

**04. The shuttles run **after dark****
  EN  Each academy operates its own small bus. Near ten at night they queue nose to tail along the street with their hazard lights on, parents idling behind them, and the pavement fills for about fifteen minutes.

**05. ₩27.5tn — What the country **spent on it in one year****
  EN  Roughly $19bn on private tutoring for school children. Across all children that is ₩458,000 a month; a high schooler who actually attends averages ₩793,000.

**06. So the law **sends them home****
  EN  Since 2008, local ordinances have capped how late an academy may teach. Seoul, Gyeonggi, Daegu and Gwangju stop at 10pm, Busan and Incheon at 11, Daejeon and Ulsan at midnight. Education offices send out night patrols before exams to check.

**07. The cap has **a door in it****
  EN  A study cafe is not an academy, so no cap applies. At 10:01pm children move to one and keep going, and a paid self-study industry has grown into exactly the gap the rule left open.

**08. Whether 10pm is right **is still argued****
  EN  Seoul has been weighing letting high schoolers study until midnight. Academy associations are for it and parents are split. The argument is over a rule that exists because, before it, the hours had no end at all.

**09. And nobody calls it **normal****
  EN  Last year the total fell for the first time in five years and participation dropped four points. It was reported as a warning, not a win: fewer children in academies, more money spent on each one who stays.

  EN  Follow for the context behind the things Korea does.
