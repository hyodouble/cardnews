## 확인이 필요한 사실

1. Entry price ₩10,000-17,000: sampled from venue price lists in Sept 2026 (e.g. 강변스파랜드 lists sauna ₩13,000 / jjimjilbang ₩15,000 for 05:00-20:00 and ₩17,000 for 20:00-05:00). It is a range, not a national figure, and the cards say 'roughly'. Re-check one Seoul venue before publishing if a sharper number is wanted.
2. '₩15,000' in the hook and caption is the middle of that range, not a surveyed average. Do not turn it into 'the average price'.
3. Seoul 1,764 bathhouses in 1995 to 510 in 2025 (-71%): 시사저널 30 Mar 2026, from the reporter's own full analysis of 3,970 Seoul 목욕장업 licence records. It is published press work rather than an official statistic — attribute it that way if anyone asks. National figures from the same licence data (행정안전부 지방행정인허가데이터, analysed by 한국일보 Jan 2023): a 2003 peak of 9,919 open nationwide, down to 6,012 at the end of 2022.
4. The stat counts 목욕장업 (bathhouses) as a whole, which includes jjimjilbang. The slide says 'bathhouses' and claims nothing about jjimjilbang alone. Do not relabel the number as a jjimjilbang count.
5. 'The small neighbourhood tubs are the ones closing' is the framing in the 시사저널 piece and in earlier reporting on the 2004-2008 shakeout, when many 동네 목욕탕 converted to jjimjilbang. It is press observation, not a broken-out statistic. Do not extend it into a claim that big complexes are growing.
6. 24-hour operation: many jjimjilbang run overnight and rent a sleeping room, but not all do, and some dropped overnight hours after COVID. The card says 'many' and mentions a night rate. Keep both hedges.
7. Nude sex-segregated baths and a clothed mixed jjimjil floor is how the format is built, not a survey result.
8. Sleeping-room fittings vary — mats plus wooden or plastic headrests are the common case. The card says 'hard headrests' to avoid naming one material.
9. Saturday as a family outing is written as an observation with no number attached. No attendance breakdown was found; do not add a percentage.
10. Sesin (세신), the scrub in the reply, is a separately paid service and prices vary by venue, so the reply names no price. No claim is made about health benefits of the scrub or of the kiln rooms — keep it that way.

## 슬라이드 문구

**01. Koreans sleep on the floor next to **strangers** — and bring the kids.**
  EN  SATURDAY, ONE FLOOR, FORTY STRANGERS
  EN  For about ₩15,000, and the building never closes.

**02. It's called a **jjimjilbang (찜질방)****
  EN  A bathhouse with heated rooms attached. One ticket buys the whole building, roughly ₩10,000-17,000. Many run 24 hours, with a night rate after evening.

**03. **Two floors**, two sets of rules**
  EN  The baths are nude and split by sex. The jjimjil floor above is clothed and mixed, which is the part visitors get wrong.

**04. Everyone wears **the same outfit****
  EN  Shirt and shorts come with the ticket. People fold the towel into sheep horns (양머리). A director and a student look identical.

**05. Heat is treated as **rest****
  EN  Kiln rooms run from warm to barely bearable. You cycle through them, then lie flat on the floor until you fall asleep.

**06. It's also **a bed for the night****
  EN  Miss the last train and this beats a hotel. The sleeping room has mats and hard headrests, and nobody asks why you're there.

**07. Here's what **outsiders miss****
  EN  Elsewhere a spa is a treat you book. Here it's closer to a utility — bath, nap, meal, and the option to not go home.

**08. 1,764 → 510 — Seoul's bathhouses, **1995 to 2025****
  EN  Seven in ten are gone in thirty years. The small neighbourhood tubs are the ones closing, one lease at a time.

**09. So it isn't **a spa day****
  EN  It's being warm, fed and horizontal in public, in the same clothes as everyone else, and nobody there finds that strange.

**10. Korea, explained **by a Korean.****
  EN  Follow for the context behind the things Korea does.
