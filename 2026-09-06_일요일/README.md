## 캡션 — 영어판 (그대로 복사)

```
In Korea a government document comes out of a machine in the subway station, not from a clerk behind a counter. Feed it your ID card, press a fingerprint, and the residence record prints stamped and sealed in under a minute.

The kiosks sit in supermarkets and station concourses, and the same paperwork can be pulled up on Gov24 and printed at home instead. What outsiders read as extreme convenience is really one decision: every resident sits under a single registration number, so no office has to ask twice who you are.

And it is not built only for citizens. A registered foreign resident gets a certificate of alien registration through the same offices and the same portal, and the law says it stands in wherever a Korean ID or residence record is demanded.
```

## 확인이 필요한 사실

1. 'Gov24 passed 20 million registered users in April 2023, about a third of the population': 뉴스핌 19 Apr 2023, '정부24 회원 2000만명 돌파 눈앞…국민 3명 중 1명 사용', citing 행정안전부. The card dates the figure to April 2023 outright rather than saying 'now' — the same drift that had to be corrected on the 2026-09-04 carousel. Re-check before reusing this number on a later card.
2. 'A residence record costs nothing that way': 주민등록 등·초본 issued online through 정부24 is free. Confirmed on gov.kr's own guidance.
3. The kiosk fee is deliberately left off the slides. Sources disagree — some report a nationwide move to free issuance from 1 Jan 2026, others still list 300원 with certain districts waiving it. gov.kr states only that '무인민원발급기 발급시간 및 발급수수료는 자치단체 무인민원발급창구 운영시간에 따라 달라질 수 있습니다', so the reply says fees are set locally and names no figure. Do not put a single national fee on a card without a 행정안전부 primary source.
4. Kiosk operating hours are also set locally, per the same gov.kr line. The hook says 'no office hours' about the service as a whole, and slide 5 puts the genuinely 24-hour claim on Gov24 online, not on the machines. Do not move a '24 hours' claim onto the kiosks.
5. 'Dozens of documents': municipal lists (서울 중구, 영등포구, 도봉구, 인천시) run to residence records, family relation certificates, 토지·임야대장, 건축물대장, 개별공시지가확인원, 자동차등록원부, 병적증명서 and more. 'Dozens' is safe; a specific count is not — the list differs by machine and by local government.
6. 'You feed it your ID card, press a fingerprint': fingerprint verification is the standard route for 주민등록등본 at these kiosks. Verify against a current 행정안전부 or municipal guide before publishing, since some machines now also take mobile ID.
7. 'Registered foreign residents get a certificate of alien registration from the same portal': 외국인등록사실증명, service page on gov.kr (CappBizCD=12700000027). 출입국관리법 puts issuance with the 출입국관리사무소장·출장소장 or the 시장·군수·구청장 or 읍·면·동장 — the same counter, which is why the slide says 'the same portal' and 'the same offices'.
8. 'By law it stands in where a Korean ID is demanded': the rule is that where a statute or a transaction requires a 주민등록증 or 주민등록등·초본, the 외국인등록증 or 외국인등록사실증명서 substitutes for it. Quoted from the 민원편람 wording carried by local governments (고양시 등). Confirm the current 출입국관리법 clause before this line goes out.
9. The slide deliberately does NOT say foreign residents can use the kiosk itself. Whether a 무인민원발급기 accepts an 외국인등록증 was not confirmed — official pages describe fingerprint verification for 주민등록 holders and say nothing about registered foreigners, and support looks to vary by machine and local government. Do not upgrade this to 'the same machine' without a 행정안전부 or 출입국·외국인청 source.
10. The online route needing a digital certificate (reply): gov.kr states 외국인등록 사실증명 online issuance is available only with a certificate confirming identity. That friction is left in on purpose — it is what keeps the carousel from reading as a brochure.
11. 'Every resident has a single 13-digit registration number': 주민등록번호 is 13 digits. Structural fact, not a survey result.
12. Slide 7 makes no numeric claim about any other country's waiting times on purpose. No comparable cross-country statistic was checked, so it describes the shape of the process abroad, not a measured delay.
13. Earlier precedent, not on the cards: the November 2023 국가행정망 전산마비 took 정부24 down for most of a day. It was the first draft's shadow slide and was replaced because the 2025 fire is more recent, better sourced and tells the sharper story. Korean coverage explicitly linked the two ('2년 전 행정전산망 마비에서 배운 것 없나', 보안뉴스). Do not put both on one carousel.
14. Dropped shadow slide, recorded so it is not re-added by accident: slide 9 was twice a disaster story — the November 2023 국가행정망 마비, then the 2025-09-26 국가정보자원관리원 대전센터 fire (647 systems down, 정부24 restored 29 Sep, all 693 Daejeon systems back after 49 days). Both were well sourced. Both were cut for scale: daeri unjeon's ninth card carried a chronic quiet risk, and a burning data centre is not that. If a shadow is wanted here later, it belongs in the reply, not on a slide.

## 슬라이드 문구

**01. In Korea an official document comes out of a **machine in the subway station.****
  EN  NO APPOINTMENT. NO CLERK. NO OFFICE HOURS.
  EN  Residence record, family register, land title — printed, stamped and sealed while you stand there.

**02. It has a name: **muin minwon balgeupgi****
  EN  Muin minwon balgeupgi (무인민원발급기) — the unmanned civil affairs kiosk. You feed it your ID card, press a fingerprint, and it prints the certified document.

**03. They sit **where you already are****
  EN  Not only at the district office. Subway stations, large supermarkets, community centres. The counter was moved onto the route people already walk.

**04. It prints the **paperwork of a whole life****
  EN  Residence records, family relation certificates, land and building registers, vehicle records, proof of military service. Dozens of documents, one machine.

**05. Or you skip the trip **entirely****
  EN  Gov24 (정부24) sends the same documents to a home printer. A residence record costs nothing that way, and the site does not close at five.

**06. One number makes it **possible****
  EN  Every resident has a single 13-digit registration number. Agencies read one record, so the machine never asks you to prove who you are twice.

**07. Here's what **outsiders miss****
  EN  In most countries this is an appointment, a queue and a working day spent. Korea decided the counter did not need a person behind it.

**08. 1 in 3 — Koreans had already **signed up** to the portal**
  EN  Gov24 passed 20 million registered users in April 2023, about a third of the population — before counting anyone who only ever uses the kiosks.

**09. If you **live here**, you're in it too**
  EN  Registered foreign residents get a certificate of alien registration from the same portal. By law it stands in where a Korean ID is demanded.

**10. Korea, explained **by a Korean.****
  EN  Follow for the context behind the things Korea does.
