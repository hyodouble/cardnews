# Why Koreans End a Date at a Photo Machine

## 캡션 — 영어판 (그대로 복사)

```
In Korea a night out does not end at the last bar. It ends at a photo booth, in a shop with no staff in it, at about ₩5,000 for four small pictures.

It is called insaeng nekeot — "four cuts of your life" — after the brand that started it in 2017 and ended up naming the whole category. You pick a frame, the machine counts down six to eight times while you pose against a plain wall, and you keep four. It prints two copies, one for each of you, and that is the part that matters.

What surprises visitors is how little it edits. Japanese purikura enlarges the eyes and sharpens the chin; the Korean booth mostly lights you properly, drains the colour, and prints. Plenty of the strips people treasure are black and white.

Then it goes on a wall. A board by the door of a cafe, a grid beside the light switch at home, or behind a clear phone case. The QR code on the paper expires in a day or a week, so the paper usually outlives the file.

Nobody does it for the photograph. You met, so there should be proof.
```

## 확인이 필요한 사실

1. 'insaeng nekeot / 2017' (2장): 인생네컷 브랜드가 2017년에 시작해 셀프사진관 전체를 가리키는 보통명사처럼 쓰이게 됐다는 것까지만 카드에 썼다. 법인(엘케이벤처스) 설립은 2018년이라 두 연도가 같지 않으니 '브랜드 시작'으로만 써야 한다.
2. 'no staff at all' (2장·6장): 대부분 무인이지만 상주 직원을 두는 매장도 있다. 카드는 'usually'로 완화해 썼다.
3. 'six to eight shots' (3장): 브랜드·상품별로 4·6·8컷 찍기가 다르다. 단일 숫자로 단정하지 않고 범위로 썼다.
4. 'purikura widens the eyes' (4장): 일본 푸리쿠라가 눈·털·피부 보정을 강하게 건다는 것은 널리 알려진 비교지만, 기종별 편차가 크고 한국 부스도 보정 강도를 고를 수 있는 기종이 있다. 정도의 차이로 읽히게 썼다.
5. '₩4,000~6,000 + ₩1,000~3,000 frame' (5장): 기본 네컷 4,000~6,000원, 프레임 추가 1,000~3,000원이라는 2026년 유통 정보 기준. 브랜드·지역별 편차가 있어 후크에서는 ₩5,000 한 점만 박았다.
6. 'prints two copies' (5장): 기본이 2매 인화가 맞지만 인원이 많으면 추가 인화를 돈 내고 뽑는다. 카드는 둘이 간 경우를 전제로 쓴 문장이다.
7. 'props are free / it is all still there' (6장): 소품을 무료로 내놓는 것은 사실이다. 다만 **무인점포 절도·기물파손은 실제로 있는 문제**라, '아무것도 안 없어진다'로 단정하지 않았다. 9/1 자리맡기 편과 같은 주장을 반복하지 않기 위해서도 약하게 썼다.
8. 'QR expires in 24 hours to a week' (8장): 브랜드별 원본 보관 기간이 24시간~7일로 알려져 있다. 전 브랜드 공통 규칙은 아니므로 'usually'로 썼다.
9. '셀프사진관 400개점 넘기고 감소' (첫 댓글): 2021년 239개 → 2022년 401개 → 2023년 383개 → 이후 330개대라는 보도 기준. 집계 범위(브랜드 가맹점만인지 전체 시장인지)가 자료마다 달라 카드가 아닌 댓글에만, 숫자 하나만 썼다.

## 슬라이드 문구

**01. Korea ends its nights out **in a photo booth.****
  EN  SATURDAY NIGHT. HONGDAE. THE LAST STOP IS NOT A BAR.
  EN  Four small pictures, about ₩5,000, printed twice so you each take one home.

**02. It has a name: **insaeng nekeot****
  EN  "Four cuts of your life." It began as one brand in 2017 and turned into the word for the whole thing, the way a tissue became a Kleenex. There is no photographer and usually no staff at all — you walk in, feed the machine, and it runs the session.

**03. You get **eight tries and four keeps****
  EN  Pick a frame, and the machine counts you down six to eight times, a few seconds apart, while you pose against a plain wall. At the end you choose the four that make the strip. The rest are gone.

**04. It barely **retouches you****
  EN  This is what surprises anyone raised on Japanese purikura, which widens the eyes and sharpens the chin until the face is a cartoon. The Korean booth mostly lights you well, pulls the colour out, and prints. Half the strips people love are black and white.

**05. ₩5,000 — The whole price of **the memory****
  EN  A basic strip runs ₩4,000 to ₩6,000, plus ₩1,000 to ₩3,000 for a licensed frame — a K-pop group, a cartoon, the shop's own design. It prints two copies. One each. That split is the point.

**06. The props are **left out for you****
  EN  A wall of hats, sunglasses, headbands and hand-held signs sits by the door, free to grab, next to a rack of hair irons and mirrors. Nobody signs anything out and nobody is watching the room. It is all still there for the next pair.

**07. Then it goes **on a wall****
  EN  Cafes and bars keep a board by the door for customers' strips. Couples build a grid of them at home beside the light switch, and a clear phone case is half photo-holder — the strip is cut to the size that slides in behind it.

**08. The digital copy **expires****
  EN  A QR code on the paper gives you the files, and the shops usually hold the originals for 24 hours to a week. Save them that night or the printed strip is the only copy you own.

**09. It is a **receipt for the day****
  EN  Ask why and nobody answers photography. It is the closing move: you met, so there should be proof. A first date, a last day at work, a friend flying out tomorrow — all of them finish at the same machine.

**10. Korea, explained **by a Korean.****
  EN  Follow for the context behind the things Korea does.
