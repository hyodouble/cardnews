## 슬라이드 문구

**01. In Korea the delivery rider **never sees your face.****
  EN  NO DOORBELL. NO CASH. NO WORDS EXCHANGED.
  EN  He leaves the bag at your door, texts a photo, and is gone before you open it.

**02. It has a name: **bidaemyeon baedal****
  EN  Bidaemyeon baedal (비대면 배달) — contactless delivery. It's a checkbox at checkout, not a special request you have to make.

**03. You tell the rider **exactly where to leave it****
  EN  Apartment number, floor, 'by the elevator,' the gate code — the delivery note field takes all of it, so nobody has to buzz you first.

**04. A photo **closes the loop****
  EN  The app pushes a picture of the bag on your doormat the second it lands, so you know it arrived before you even open the door.

**05. Nobody's tipping, and that's **the point****
  EN  There's no tip field in the app. The delivery fee is fixed and already paid, so the rider isn't lingering at your door hoping for more.

**06. A rating does the job a tip usually does**
  EN  Every order ends with a star rating and a written review of the delivery. That's the accountability, not cash changing hands at the door.

**07. Where you live, this might be the rude part**
  EN  In plenty of countries the rider waits for a signature, a chat, or cash. In Korea skipping that exchange entirely is the polite move, not the cold one.

**08. 54% — One app **eats over half the market****
  EN  Baemin (배달의민족) holds roughly 54% of Korea's delivery-app orders as of early 2026, with Coupang Eats a fast-closing second near 29%.

**09. One swap replaced the whole interaction**
  EN  A location note, a photo, and a fee that's already paid — that's what stands in for the knock, the wait and the tip.
